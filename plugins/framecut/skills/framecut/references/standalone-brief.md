# Standalone brief

Use this when FrameCut runs without Creative Studio, or when Studio sent no brief. When Studio sent a task envelope, its brief, copy locks and board acceptance are the inputs and this file is skipped.

Inspect what the user supplied first (duration, streams, speech, language, aspect) and ask only for what the files cannot tell, one question at a time. Stop asking as soon as the edit can be planned. A wrong guess that is cheap to fix is a stated assumption, not a question.

1. **Result and viewer.** What the film must make the viewer know, feel or do, who watches it and where (platform, screen).
2. **Material.** Which footage, recordings, photos, music or voice-over are available, what is missing, and the right to use them.
3. **Outputs.** Length, aspect ratios (9:16, 1:1, 16:9 or several), number of cuts or highlights, subtitle files or burned-in captions, delivery format.
4. **Must keep and exact copy.** Names, numbers, claims, quotes and steps that must stay, and every on-screen word, verbatim, with its language.
5. **Look and sound.** Caption style, titles, pace, music under speech or none, voice-over. With no direction given, propose one from the material instead of asking brand questions.

Then write the brief as five short lines (result, material, outputs, must keep, look and sound), mark each item Confirmed, Proposed or Unknown, and continue with the storyboard in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md#storyboard-before-final-rendering): the whole film as contact sheets before any final render. For an open brief, give two or three different directions drawn from the material's own moments, people and sound, recommend one, and ask one easy question that separates them.

Ownership without Studio: FrameCut keeps the brief, the idea, the copy locks, the storyboard acceptance and the delivery itself. Links in this skill marked "Creative Studio: `...`" point to Studio files that exist only when Studio is installed; without it, follow the rule written next to the link and skip the file.
